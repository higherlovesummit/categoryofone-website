The Brand Strategy Guide

Brand Strategy: The Science of Creating an Authentic Brand

Brand Strategy is the first step to building a brand — it builds the foundation and guides every downstream decision and activity your business will make.

You most likely have a business strategy. You may even have a marketing strategy. But what sits in between the two is your Brand Strategy.

Skipping ahead to marketing strategy without a Brand Strategy is the most common mistake business owners make. We refer to Brand Strategy as “The Science of the Branding Process.”

Understanding Brand Strategy can be overwhelming, which is why we created this guide to teach you the fundamentals. While not exhaustive, it should give you a starting point in the world of strategy.

Your Brand Strategy defines and articulates several layers of your brand — the nine building blocks below.

01

Target Audiences

Here's the obvious: if you're going to have a business, you'll need customers. You'll also need to understand what those customers look like and what is going on in their minds.

This begins by answering three specific questions:

  1. Who are your target audiences?
  2. What are the problems you solve for your target audiences?
  3. Why should your target audiences listen to, buy from, or recommend you?

While simple questions, answering them is key to uncovering everything else in your Brand Strategy.

Who are your target audiences?

To get more clarity, we recommend creating a demographic profile (factors such as age, race, gender, income, and location) and a psychographic profile (your customer's fears, frustrations, wants, aspirations, lifestyle, etc.) of your customers.

What are the problems you solve for your target audiences?

All businesses and brands should solve people's problems. Knowing the problems you solve will inform your messaging and go-to-market approach. These are often defined by their functional, economic, and emotional utility to customers.

Why should your target audiences listen to, buy from, or recommend you?

Your audience is likely very savvy and skeptical, so you have to be smart about how you appeal to them. It's not enough to say you are great at what you do. This is why we recommend using Proof Points — specific examples of your experience, list of clients, track record of success, and feedback from previous clients. All of this helps overcome any skepticism they may have about your company.

02

Brand Purpose

Your Brand Purpose is a descriptive and aspirational articulation of why your brand exists.

Think of your Brand Purpose as a statement that explains why you created your company and the direction it is headed. It should be short, simple, and articulate the personality of your brand.

03

Brand Archetypes

Archetypes are a concept originally conceived by famed Swiss psychiatrist Carl Jung. When used in branding and marketing, the idea is referred to as Brand Archetypes.

Essentially, Brand Archetypes are used by branding professionals as a heuristic to infuse personality into a company's brand. A Brand Archetype is identified when you fill in the blank to the following statement: If our company were a person, that person would be described as __________. It's essential to identify your Brand Archetypes based on where the brand is heading — not where it has historically been.

04

Brand Architecture

Your Brand Architecture is a strategic framework that organizes your offerings and determines how to brand (or not brand) them from a verbal and visual perspective.

There are four broad frameworks for Brand Architecture: Monolithic, Endorsed, Freestanding, and Hybrid.

05

The Competitive Landscape

Depending on your appetite to understand your competitors, you'll want to conduct a review of three to seven competitors. The sweet spot is your top five. This process is relatively easy, as it involves primarily desk research — reviewing your competitors' websites, social media, and other marketing channels.

There are six key competitor elements to explore:

  1. What does their logo look like?
  2. What colors do they use?
  3. How would you describe the overall look and feel of their branding and designs?
  4. What do they seem to talk most about?
  5. What does the voice of their brand sound like?
  6. What appears to be their overall Brand Positioning?

06

Brand Positioning

Brand Positioning is the space you occupy (or intend to occupy) in your target audiences' minds. This is what we call the “white space” in your industry or category — the place in your marketplace where no one else is. Taking ownership of this space is what helps differentiate you from your competitors.

There are many ways to “position” your brand, all depending on which variables you believe will help you differentiate. You can position around a word or concept, through color, or even through time.

To help find the white space in your industry, create a Brand Positioning Map — it enables you to visualize how your brand is unique in the competitive landscape.

07

Brand Promise

Your Brand Promise is the ultimate benefit that your customers or clients will receive when buying your products or services. It guides everything about your brand — from how you look, to how you speak, to the customer experience.

We recommend articulating your Brand Promise in fourteen words or less. The shorter, the better. Why? When your Brand Promise is concise, it's easy to remember — and memorability is key in branding. Plus, it will be easier to implement.

Avoid lengthy Brand Promise statements in an attempt to include everything. You don't need to put the responsibility on your Brand Promise to communicate everything.

08

Brand Platform

Once you've identified your Brand Promise, the next step is to dimensionalize it into four key components:

Brand Values — the guiding principles of behavior that deliver on the brand. Brand Values are distinct from personal or core values such as integrity, honesty, or trust (which are table stakes). They may overlap, but the critical distinction is that Brand Values are specific to your company's brand.

Brand Attributes — the personality characteristics of the brand. These should complement the chosen Brand Archetype(s) and are typically adjectives. If you're unsure, ask whether you'd use that word to describe a person's personality.

Brand Voice — the brand's sound, expressed to verbalize tone and manner in language. Brand Voice helps a copywriter capture how copy should be written. If unsure, ask whether you'd use that word to describe how a person sounds when they speak.

Value Propositions — the key day-to-day tangible or intangible benefits the brand provides, ranging from functional to economic to emotional. Where target audiences speak to your audience's problems, Value Propositions speak to the benefits and solutions you offer.

09

Messaging Matrix

The final step in your Brand Strategy. “Messaging” refers to an organizing framework for all the messages used in branding and marketing communications. A “message” is simply a key communication point — not necessarily customer-facing language, but the crux of the main points your brand wants to make.

Some examples of messages:

  • Safety
  • Reliability
  • Accessibility
  • Expertise
  • Social Impact

Your messaging matrix serves as a pillar for all your communications. Messaging can be segmented in many ways depending on your needs — by target audience, value proposition, or a host of other approaches.

Thank you for reading this guide to Brand Strategy. We hope it’s given you a strong overview of what goes into creating one. Of course, this is just the beginning — once your Strategy is complete, you’ll have everything you need to step into your Brand Identity, both visual and verbal.
